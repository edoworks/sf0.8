# AI-Era Learning Opportunity Discovery

Status: evidence audit complete, five candidate hypotheses remain unvalidated
Owner: Foculoom factory
Date: 2026-09-19
Authority: map issue #50. This document is a research artifact, not product
authorization. No education product is admitted to active work.

## Decision Summary

Current recommendation: run only the cheapest falsification experiments and do
not build an education platform, tutor, chatbot, or LMS. No concept is a
validated opportunity.

1. **Mastery proof for one narrow concept**: a local, visual interaction that
   makes a learner predict, manipulate, explain, and demonstrate one concept.
2. **Parent-child explanation bridge**: a parent chooses a real homework
   friction and the child and parent conduct a short, non-answer-giving,
   observable learning exchange.

Teacher augmentation, curiosity discovery, and AI-literacy concepts remain
research candidates but have insufficient Customer Zero and willingness-to-pay
evidence. Child-facing open-ended AI is rejected for the first experiment due
to safety burden and weak proof that conversation produces learning.

This is an internal experiment-selection inference, not proof of customer pain,
learning effect, willingness to pay, or product-market fit.

## Claim Discipline

- **FACT**: directly supported by a cited source or observed factory record.
- **INFERENCE**: a reasoned interpretation of facts.
- **HYPOTHESIS**: must be tested with a named customer and measurable gate.
- **PROPOSAL**: a factory decision that is not evidence of demand.

## Gap -> Evidence -> Loophole -> Product Concept

### Gap 1: trustworthy demonstration of understanding

- **Gap**: free explanation and practice are abundant, but a parent or learner
  may not know whether the learner can transfer a concept without hints.
- **Evidence**: Khan describes instant feedback and mastery; WWC shows that
  credible learning claims require evaluation; the Khan review corpus contains
  an example of trust damage from a possible grading/content error.
- **Loophole**: use a small set of deterministic, manipulable tasks and require
  prediction, explanation, transfer and delayed re-test instead of generating
  more answers.
- **Concept**: **Proof Tiles**, an Apple-native visual mastery check for one
  narrow concept at a time. No open chat. Local item bank, audio optional,
  explain-by-building response.
- **Status**: strongest evidence-backed opportunity, still HYPOTHESIS for
  willingness to pay and learning transfer.

### Gap 2: homework help that lowers family conflict without doing the work

- **Gap**: parents may be asked to supervise concepts they cannot explain, but
  the direct pain is unverified in this factory.
- **Evidence**: no direct family interviews. UNESCO's age-appropriate and
  human-centred guardrails make an answer-giving child chatbot a poor default.
- **Loophole**: help the parent ask a productive question and help the child
  externalize a prediction; never supply the homework answer.
- **Concept**: **Side-by-Side Spark**, a parent-started, child-readable visual
  activity with a 10-minute shared outcome and a parent summary of what was
  demonstrated.
- **Status**: test only after recruiting a family with a recent homework
  conflict; not yet a build candidate.

### Gap 3: teacher time spent adapting and checking understanding

- **Gap**: teachers report stress and technology burden; adaptation and
  feedback are plausible high-cost jobs.
- **Evidence**: RAND supports the workload signal. No buyer interview or
  current procurement evidence exists.
- **Loophole**: generate differentiated, teacher-approved probes from a fixed
  curriculum slice, then summarize misconception patterns without autonomous
  grading.
- **Concept**: **Misconception Lens**, a teacher-owned local assessment review
  tool.
- **Status**: research candidate; B2B buying cycle and integration cost make it
  lower priority than a direct narrow experiment.

### Gap 4: curiosity-to-curriculum connection

- **Gap**: a learner's interest may not connect to a standards-aligned next
  action; loss of curiosity is unverified here.
- **Evidence**: Khan and Duolingo show broad content discovery and high ratings;
  no evidence says discovery is the binding pain.
- **Loophole**: start from a learner-created question, constrain the path to a
  verifiable artifact, and expose uncertainty and sources.
- **Concept**: **Question Garden**, a visual inquiry trail with no generative
  answer feed.
- **Status**: weak evidence; do not build before interviews.

### Gap 5: safe AI literacy and critical reasoning

- **Gap**: learners need to distinguish fluent output from reliable evidence;
  exact customer and payment trigger are unverified.
- **Evidence**: UNESCO identifies validation, privacy and age-appropriate use as
  policy gaps; no local user evidence exists.
- **Loophole**: let learners compare two deliberately different claims,
  inspect provenance, predict which is trustworthy, and justify a choice.
- **Concept**: **Source Detective**, a deterministic claim-comparison game
  using curated examples before any model-generated content.
- **Status**: promising educational mechanism, weak commercial evidence.

## Learning Proof

### Common gate

`BEFORE -> EXPERIENCE -> AFTER`

- **Before**: learner independently predicts or solves a transfer item; record
  confidence and misconception, not just a score.
- **Experience**: learner manipulates a representation, tests a prediction,
  compares outcomes, explains a choice, and receives bounded feedback.
- **After**: learner independently solves a novel but isomorphic item and
  explains the rule. Re-test after 48 hours where practical.

### Automated validation

- Deterministic item bank with preregistered answer and misconception keys.
- UI tests verify the learner must act before revealing explanation.
- Event log checks prediction, manipulation, explanation and transfer steps;
  events are local and pseudonymous.
- Score transfer accuracy, hint count, time-to-first-prediction, and delayed
  retention; never use session duration as a learning proxy.
- Snapshot tests for visual states, accessibility traversal and textless core
  flow.
- Safety tests for prohibited open-ended child interaction, data export and
  network access.

### Human/customer validation

- Observe the named Customer Zero using the first prototype with no coaching.
- Blind-score the before and after explanation with a simple rubric.
- Interview immediately about the workaround, trust, conflict, and payment
  trigger; distinguish praise from commitment.
- Ask for a real payment, refundable deposit, referral, or repeated scheduled
  use. “Would you pay?” is not enough.
- For early-childhood concepts, test whether a four-year-old can begin,
  predict, manipulate and recover from an error without reading instructions or
  adult explanation.
- Independent educator review is required before any educational effectiveness
  claim.

## Safety and Privacy Gate

The first experiment should be local, deterministic, no account, no ads, no
open chat, no child voice upload, and no model/network dependency. If a model is
later introduced:

- parent-controlled setup and explicit AI disclosure;
- minimum age and age-appropriate interaction design;
- no persuasive streaks, emotional dependency, or anthropomorphic authority;
- allowlist output or structured generation with refusal tests;
- no training on child data; collect only events needed for the learning proof;
- human review of content and escalation path for unsafe output;
- clear uncertainty and no diagnosis, grading, or educational-effectiveness
  claims without evidence;
- deletion/export and parental controls tested on device;
- threat-model prompt injection, data exfiltration, hallucination, and network
  outage before a child-facing pilot.

## Kill Criteria

Kill or pause an experiment when any of the following is true:

- 5 recruited Customer Zeros produce no recent incident, workaround, or
  observable pain.
- Fewer than 3 of 5 learners improve on the preregistered transfer task, or
  improvement disappears on delayed re-test.
- Parents/teachers prefer free alternatives and no one pays, deposits, refers,
  or schedules repeated use after two attempts.
- The experience gives answers faster than it causes prediction, manipulation,
  explanation or demonstration.
- A child cannot understand the core interaction without adult explanation,
  where child independence is part of the concept.
- Safety review finds unbounded child-model interaction, unnecessary child data,
  persuasive dependency, or unverifiable educational claims.
- Content production/support cost makes the observed price uneconomic.

## Recommended Experiments

### E1: Proof Tiles concierge validation

Customer Zero: Maya, the homeschooling parent described above. Recruit 5
people matching this exact story only after Maya validates the wording.

1. Run a paper/prototype fraction transfer task before instruction.
2. Give a 10-minute visual manipulation sequence with no AI.
3. Run a novel transfer task and 48-hour re-test.
4. Ask the parent to pay $9.99 for the next concept pack or make a refundable
   deposit.

Gate: at least 3/5 improve on transfer, 3/5 complete without adult rescue, and
2/5 pay or deposit. Otherwise revise or kill.

### E2: Side-by-Side Spark family observation

Customer Zero: Daniel, the parent whose homework conflict is recent and
documented. Do not recruit generic parents.

1. Observe one real homework friction without intervention.
2. Replace explanation with a parent-child visual probe.
3. Record conflict duration, child independent explanation, and parent trust.
4. Test a $14.99 purchase or referral to another family.

Gate: conflict duration decreases without answer leakage; child can explain the
concept; parent repeats the activity within two weeks; at least 2/5 pay or
deposit. Otherwise do not build a family product.

### E3: Misconception Lens interview, not build

Customer Zero: Priya, a named teacher with a recent exit-ticket sorting burden.
Ask for a real anonymized artifact and time estimate. No student data enters
the factory. Proceed to prototype only if five teachers independently show the
same repeated burden and two accept a paid pilot.

## Portfolio Decision

Evidence currently supports **several independent experiments**, not a platform
or reusable education capability layer. A single narrowly scoped product may be
earned if E1 or E2 passes its learning and payment gates. “No education
product” remains a live outcome if direct users reject the pains or free
alternatives dominate. Broader platform progression requires two successful,
independent concepts sharing a demonstrated capability and a second consumer;
nothing here authorizes that extraction.

## Open Questions and Unsupported Assumptions

- Is homework conflict frequent and expensive enough for a parent to pay?
- Does a visual mastery proof change parent decisions or only create another
  score?
- Will children tolerate deliberate prediction before seeing answers?
- Will teachers provide artifacts and pay outside procurement cycles?
- Does curiosity discovery produce more completed, retained work than search?
- Can any AI component improve the measured outcome after inference cost,
  moderation and privacy burden?
- Are the cited public reviews representative of current versions and the
  target age group? Unknown.
- No willingness-to-pay evidence exists for any concept. Unknown.

## Red-Team Disposition

The five named concepts are not “surviving opportunities.” Proof Tiles,
Side-by-Side Spark, and Misconception Lens are **candidate hypotheses** with
cheap validation experiments. Source Detective and Question Garden are
**PARKED** because no recent incident, reachable buyer, observed workaround,
or payment behavior justifies active validation. None is `VALIDATED`.

Every experiment must leave a structured record containing the observed pain,
current workaround, switching behavior, strongest monetization behavior,
learning-effect evidence, failed assumptions, reusable interaction patterns,
safety findings, and methodology. A failed experiment is reusable knowledge;
it is not silently converted into customer rejection or absence of evidence.
